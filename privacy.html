QuikSnap for iOS

Privacy Policy

Effective October 10, 2026 · Published by Oleg Shportko

In short: QuikSnap takes photos and uploads them to a Google Drive folder your team chooses. The photos go directly from your phone to Google Drive. We do not run a server, we do not see your photos, and we do not sell or share any data.

What QuikSnap does

QuikSnap is a camera app for field teams. You pick a project folder in Google Drive, take photos, and the app uploads each photo to that folder. The 2-in-1 and 3-in-1 modes stitch several shots into a single image before uploading.

Google Drive access

When you sign in, Google asks you to allow QuikSnap to access Google Drive. The app uses that access to list folders, create project folders, and upload photos. QuikSnap does not read, modify, or delete files other than the folders and photos it manages for you.

QuikSnap's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

You can remove QuikSnap's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, or by tapping Sign out in the app's Settings.

What we do not do

  • We do not operate any server. Photos and data move only between your phone and Google.
  • We do not collect analytics, crash reports, advertising identifiers, or location.
  • We do not sell, rent, or share your information with anyone.
  • We do not use your photos or data for advertising or to train machine-learning models.

Camera permission

QuikSnap asks for camera access so you can take photos. It does not access your existing photo library.

Data retention

Photos stay on the phone only until they have been uploaded; a photo that fails to upload is kept so it can be retried, and is removed after a successful upload. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the phone, including sign-in tokens. Photos already uploaded remain in your Google Drive, where you control them.

Children

QuikSnap is a work tool for adults and is not directed at children under 13.
